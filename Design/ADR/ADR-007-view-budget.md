# ADR-007 — The view budget: absolute fog distances, bounded chunk residency

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-18
**Owner:** the author, on the owner's frame of 2026-09-18 and the owner's question of the same day; supersedes the fog *range* of ADR-005 and nothing else in it

## Context

Two things arrived together on 2026-09-18.

The owner ran the build on real hardware and sent a frame. Measured over every non-background pixel, binned by screen row from the far edge to the near: the far half of the landscape is R = G = B **to one decimal place**, 63.9% of the landscape is exactly gray, 87.8% is within 8/255 of gray, and the most saturated pixel anywhere in the frame is 21/255. The Species palette the terrain had just been given was invisible. ADR-005 set the desaturation to run from 0.185 to 0.74 of the landscape's extent, which on the Small landscape is 1,516 to 6,062 world units; framing that landscape at 60 degrees puts the camera about 10,000 units out, so the *nearest* ground in the frame is already past the start and nearly all of it is past the end. The fault is structural, not a bad fraction: the fog range and the camera's distance both scale with the landscape, so they never separate, and an RTS camera's distance is set by how much map the player wants on screen, which is itself a fraction of the extent. ADR-005 took the fractions from `SpeciesLook.md` §5, where they were 1,000 and 4,000 units on maps 2,000 to 5,400 across and the camera hugged the ground. They do not survive the change of camera.

The owner also asked what happens to the frame as landscapes grow to the Frontier class, and whether the triangle budget of `TerrainPass.h` is enough on its own. ADR-005 answered the first half in advance — "the far field costs triangles instead of being fogged away; the budget, not the fog, bounds a frame" — and this ADR finds that answer half right: the budget is sound and it is not the binding constraint.

## Decision

**The fog range is absolute, in world units, and no longer scales with the landscape.** `FOG_START_WORLD_UNITS` (2,048, one chunk width) and `FOG_FULL_WORLD_UNITS` (8,192, four chunk widths, which is the Small landscape's extent) replace `FOG_START_FRACTION` and `FOG_END_FRACTION` in `Client/Lighting.h`. A landscape's size is not a parameter of the renderer's distances. On Small the numbers barely move, so the M0 capture frames stay comparable to the ones ADR-005 rests on; on Medium, Large and Frontier the fog stops stretching with the map, which is the defect the owner's frame shows.

**The desaturation has a ceiling.** `FOG_MAX_DESATURATION` (0.35) is the most of its own saturation a pixel may lose, carried to the shaders in the fourth component of `g_fog` and applied to the desaturation term only. This is the whole of the visible fix: the far field becomes muted rather than colourless, which is what atmospheric perspective does, and the Species look — a 3.58-peak orange sun whose entire purpose is a saturated rim on cliff faces (`SpeciesLook.md` §2, "colours above 1.0 are the point") — survives distance instead of being deleted by it.

The ceiling exists because the two fog functions are not interchangeable over one range, which ADR-005 missed. Linear-to-background is **self-limiting**: at an amount of 1 the surface *is* the background, so a fully fogged surface is never seen, it has vanished. Desaturation has no terminus: at an amount of 1 the terrain is still there in full detail and full contrast, with its colour removed. The same range that reads as a world fading into the void for the one reads as a slider pulled to zero for the other. `FOG_MAX_DESATURATION` is the terminus desaturation does not have. **`FogMode::LinearToColor` takes no ceiling** and still reaches 1.0, because reaching the background is what it is for and the capture's comparison depends on it.

**Nothing is culled by distance. What is bounded is residency, not the view.** `TerrainPass` builds every chunk at all four strides at construction and holds them in one vertex and one index buffer for the life of the match. That is 621 KiB a chunk, and it is what does not survive a Frontier landscape: 644 MB resident and, extrapolating the measured build, about 24 seconds of mesh building before the first frame. The triangle budget cannot see either figure. Instead:

- **The coarsest level stays resident for the whole landscape, always.** Stride 8 is 417 vertices and 1,920 indices — 10.3 KiB, **1.6%** of a chunk's four levels. Every chunk of a Frontier landscape at stride 8 is 10.3 MB and 655,360 triangles, so any chunk can always be drawn and the frame never has a hole in it.
- **Strides 1, 2 and 4 are built and kept only within a residency radius of the camera**, and released beyond it with a chunk of hysteresis. About 79 chunks are resident at a radius of 8,192, whatever the landscape's size.

Resident terrain becomes **59 MB across the four size classes** instead of 10 / 40 / 161 / 644 MB, and the load-time build becomes 1.6% of the landscape plus whatever is near the camera, without the player seeing anything change.

**What that does not do is remove the area term, and this ADR says so rather than implying otherwise.** The coarse level resident everywhere is O(area) with a small constant, and a small constant is a delay, not an escape: 10.3 MB at Frontier, **1.01 GB** at ten times Frontier's side. The decision above is right for the four size classes and wrong beyond them, which is the next section.

**The biome row carries the same change**, because a schema that keeps the old shape reintroduces the defect the moment `m2-skirmish/T8` wires biomes to the renderer. `BiomeDesc`'s `fogStartExtentHundredths` and `fogEndExtentHundredths` become `fogStartWorldUnits` and `fogEndWorldUnits` against `MAX_FOG_WORLD_UNITS` (65,536, a Frontier landscape's extent), and `fogMaxDesaturationHundredths` joins them. No `Biomes.json` is authored yet — the schema exists only in the loader and one test fixture — so this costs no migration and ADR-006's one-version rule is not engaged.

**ADR-005's "the far field is drawn" stands, and so does everything else in it.** This ADR supersedes its fog *range* and adds a ceiling to its chosen fog *function*; the lighting model, the unlit team-colour slot, the far plane scaling with the landscape, and the reversed depth are untouched, and `DEFAULT_FOG_MODE` still awaits the owner at `m0-foundation/T22`. In particular this ADR **rejects** bounding the drawn radius and fading the terrain to the horizon at its edge, which was the first answer drafted to the owner's question. It would have reproduced exactly the failure ADR-005 rejected on its two frames — a fog that removes the far end of the map, against `GameDesign.md` pillar 3 — it would have made the renderer wait on the sky (`m2-skirmish/T8`, `OpenQuestions.md` Q17) for a boundary to hide behind, and it would have bought nothing that bounded residency does not buy more cheaply.

**Beyond the four classes, nothing resident may be O(area)** (owner, 2026-09-18: a landscape ten times a Frontier one is a design target, not work for today). Two things in the decision above then have to go, and they are named here so the successor is not rediscovered:

- **The chunk grid becomes a quadtree whose depth grows with the landscape.** `CHUNK_STRIDES` is a fixed array of four, and a chunk is the coarsest thing there is, so the pyramid cannot merge chunks — only subdivide within one. Frontier already needs five levels to reach a single root and has four, which is exactly why all 1,024 of its chunks draw at the coarsest stride and nothing coarser exists: **the structure is already saturated at the largest class the game ships.** Ten times its side needs 8.3. With a quadtree the drawn and resident node count is levels × the nodes in a level's ring — O(log area) — which is roughly 50 to 100 MB at any size, and the coarse-everywhere term disappears because the root *is* the coarse level.
- **The heightfield stops being a resident `std::vector`.** At ten times Frontier's side it is 40,961 samples a side, 3.13 GB of `std::int16_t`, and the derived cell grid and eight commanders' visibility add 1.37 GB. That is 4.5 GB of simulation state before a triangle is drawn, and it is a larger wall than anything in this pass. A landscape is generated from a seed and a tile list, so heights are a pure function of a few hundred bytes and can be produced for a region rather than stored for a map; whether that is cheap enough for the simulation's random access is the question, and `m4-frontier/T1` is where it is asked. Pathing scales with it: `TechnicalDesign.md` §4.5 sizes the cluster graph at 4,096 clusters on Frontier, and ten times its side is 409,600.

Two decisions survive that target untouched, which is worth recording because they are the load-bearing ones. **ADR-002's positions hold**: `std::int32_t` at 1/256 of a world unit spans ten times a Frontier side with 12.8× to spare. **The state hash holds**: it hashes the definition — version, size class, seed, the tile list, starts and deposits — and never the heightfield, so determinism costs the same on any size of landscape.

**Non-goal, stated so it is never wired up: the renderer's distances are not line of sight.** The Fog pass of `TechnicalDesign.md` §6.2 — the full-screen composite that darkens explored-not-visible cells and blacks unexplored ones — reads the commander's visibility as the replica knows it: integer, deterministic, simulation state under `AGENTS.md` R16. The fog range, the residency radius and the frustum are floats on one client and depend on where that player happens to be looking. Nothing a player is allowed to *know* may ever be derived from them, and no later optimisation may cull geometry by visibility and feed the result back. They share the word "fog" and nothing else.

## Consequences

- The triangle budget stays and still demotes distant chunks, but it is no longer the thing that holds. It is not binding at Frontier scale — every chunk at stride 8 is 655,360 triangles against a budget of 1,000,000 — so a plan that checks only the budget on a large landscape checks nothing. `m3-multiplayer/T7`'s acceptance is corrected accordingly.
- **Draw calls become the next wall.** A Frontier landscape is 1,024 chunks and one `DrawIndexedInstanced` each before the frustum takes its share. Named here, not solved: merging the coarse level into fewer draws, or `ExecuteIndirect`, is the answer when it bites, and `m4-frontier/T1` measures it.
- Chunk meshes gain a lifetime. Today they are built once and never touched, and `TerrainPass` may hold raw offsets into two immortal buffers; with residency it needs a suballocator and a frame of latency before a newly resident level is drawn. That is the cost of this decision and it falls entirely inside `Client/TerrainPass.cpp`.
- The fog no longer tells the player anything about how big the map is. On Frontier the horizon looks exactly as it does on Small. That is intended — distance should read as distance — and the minimap carries scale.
- A biome's fog colour in `Biomes.json` is still carried for the linear mode and still unused by the default.
- What reopens this: draw calls rather than memory becoming the wall, or a measurement showing the residency radius costs more in streaming hitches than it saves in memory. **The owner's ten-times target reopens it by construction** and the section above says how; the four shipping size classes do not.

## Measurements

- **The owner's frame**, 2026-09-18, the interactive build on the owner's hardware at the vantage of `PoseFor(100)`, 2000×1246 as delivered. Every pixel whose brightest channel is 8 or more counted as landscape (399,519 of them), binned into twelve bands of rows. Per band, mean saturation as max-min of the channels: bands 1–4 measure **0.00**, band 5 measures 0.11, then 1.30, 2.11, 3.87, 5.58, 6.59, 7.11 toward the camera. Per-band mean R, G and B agree to one decimal in bands 1–4. Over the whole landscape: saturation p50 = 0, p75 = 6, p90 = 9, p99 = 12, max = 21; 63.9% of pixels at 3 or less, 87.8% at 8 or less. Value p10 = 19, p50 = 39, p90 = 51, p99 = 86. 819 pixels of 399,519 — 0.2% — exceed 120, and the brightest, (252, 247, 235) at (656, 788), is the one place the horizontal orange sun survives, at a saturation of 17.
- **The desaturation is exactly luminance-preserving**, so none of the frame's darkness is the fog's: `lerp(c, dot(c, W), t)` has luminance `(1 − t)·dot(c, W) + t·dot(c, W)·ΣW`, and `ΣW` = 0.299 + 0.587 + 0.114 = 1. Arithmetic on the shader, not a measurement.
- **The camera's distance**: framing the Small landscape's 11,585-unit diagonal at a 60-degree field of view needs 10,033 units. Arithmetic. At that distance ADR-005's range gives an amount of 1.00 at the map's centre; at half of it, 0.77.
- **Chunk residency**: a chunk's four levels are 24,164 vertices at 16 bytes and, on the built-in Small landscape, 124,671 indices at 2 bytes — 621 KiB, which is 16 × 621 KiB = 9.9 MB against the 386,624 vertices and 664,912 triangles ADR-005 measured for that landscape. Stride 8 alone is 417 vertices and 1,920 indices, 10.3 KiB, 1.6% of the four. Chunk counts are `SIZE_CLASS_CELLS` × 64 ÷ 2,048, squared: 16, 64, 256, 1,024. Resident totals are arithmetic on those.
- **Chunks within a radius of 8,192**: about 50 by area (π R² ÷ 2,048²), 79 with a chunk of hysteresis. Under the existing stride bands a full disc of that radius is 474,104 triangles — 7.1 chunks at stride 1, 21.2 at stride 2, 21.9 at stride 4 — against the 1,000,000 budget, before the frustum takes its share. Arithmetic on the bands in `TerrainPass::Draw`.
- **The build time** for a Frontier landscape is the 0.38 seconds ADR-005 measured for 16 chunks on `windows-latest`, scaled by chunk count to about 24 seconds. Extrapolation, not a measurement, and `m4-frontier/T1` replaces it with one.
- **Ten times a Frontier side** is 10,240 cells, 655,360 world units, 40,961 samples a side and 102,400 chunks. Heights are 40,961² × 2 bytes = 3.13 GB; the cell grid is 10,240² × 4 bytes = 0.39 GB; visibility is 10,240² × 1.25 bytes × 8 commanders = 0.98 GB; the coarse level for every chunk is 102,400 × 10.3 KiB = 1.01 GB. Levels to a single quadtree root is log₂(40,961 ÷ 128) = 8.3, against 5.0 for Frontier and the four that `CHUNK_STRIDES` provides. Position headroom is 2,147,483,647 ÷ (10,240 × 16,384) = 12.8. All arithmetic on the constants, and `OpenQuestions.md` Q19 asks whether the ten is on the side or on the area before any of it is committed to.
