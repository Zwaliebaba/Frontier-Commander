# ADR-005 — Fog and lighting

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Owner:** the author, on the owner's ruling of 2026-09-17 (`Design/OpenQuestions.md` R4); the fog choice is confirmed or overridden by the owner at `m0-foundation/T22`, and an override is a superseding ADR

## Context

The Species look lights the world with two directional lights and no ambient (`SpeciesLook.md` §2) and fades it to black between 1,000 and 4,000 units from the camera (§5), on maps 2,000 to 5,400 units across. `GameDesign.md` pillar 3 outranks the look: flat colour, hard silhouettes, one colour per commander that no light may tint, and a fight readable from the far end of the map. The owner ruled on 2026-09-17 (R4): zero ambient stays, team-colour slots are drawn unlit, and the fog either scales with the landscape or becomes distance desaturation, which this ADR decides on a captured frame rather than on the numbers alone, because the two look nothing alike and only a frame shows what each hides. `TechnicalDesign.md` §6.2 adds that which of the two is chosen decides how much of the far field is drawn at all, and §12 reserved this ADR for the terrain pass (`m0-foundation/T20`). The first landscape drawn is the frame.

## Decision

**Lighting is the Species model, unchanged.** Lambert only, no ambient, two directional lights whose colours may exceed one, summed and then clamped, one normal per triangle and one colour per triangle (`Client/Shaders/TerrainPS.hlsl`; `Client/Lighting.h`). The Garden's pair, a near-white key at 23 degrees and a horizontal orange sun at three and a half times white, is the built-in until `GameData\Biomes.json` carries one per biome. A face neither light reaches is black; that is the look, and the owner kept it.

**A team-colour slot is neither lit nor fogged.** A vertex whose colour alpha is `VERTEX_ALPHA_UNLIT` (0) is written as it is. Lights of up to (5.0, 2.35, 0.77) would tint every team colour orange, which the owner refused; and a fog that greys or blacks the colour at distance is worse than a tint, because the commander's colour is the one thing pillar 3 wants to survive distance. Every other vertex carries `VERTEX_ALPHA_LIT` (1). The terrain shader honours the rule though the terrain never uses the slot, so that the geometry pass of `m1-vertical-slice/K1` has a reference to copy rather than a comment to interpret.

**The fog is distance desaturation** (`FogMode::Desaturation`, `DEFAULT_FOG_MODE` in `Lighting.h`). From `FOG_START_FRACTION` (0.185) to `FOG_END_FRACTION` (0.74) of the landscape's extent, a pixel's colour blends toward its own luminance (Rec. 601 weights, in the shader) and no further: nothing darkens and nothing disappears, the far field is gray with its structure intact, and the horizon is the landscape's silhouette against the black sky. The fractions are the Species range on its 5,400-unit map, so the near fifth of a landscape is in full colour and the far quarter is gray, whatever its size: on a Small landscape (8,192 units) the ramp runs from 1,516 to 6,062 units, on a Medium (16,384) from 3,031 to 12,124, on a Large (32,768) from 6,062 to 24,248.

Why, on the frames: from the same vantage on the Small landscape, the Species fog scaled to the landscape (frame 100 of the capture) blacks out everything beyond 6,062 units, and the island's far ridge, six to ten thousand units from the camera, is gone: the band of the frame that holds the horizon is 99.4% near-black and its mean colour is (1, 1, 0). Under the desaturation (frame 200, the same vantage) the same band is 89.7% near-black with a mean of (5, 5, 5) at a saturation of 0.06, and the luminance thumbnail shows the ridge as a lighter line the linear frame does not have. The near field is identical in both, as it should be: the two lower bands agree to the pixel. Pillar 3 asks for a fight readable from the far end of the map, and a fog that removes the far end of the map cannot be squared with it; the desaturation keeps the far end readable and takes only its colour, which a team-colour slot keeps anyway. The Species mood, a world fading to nothing, is the price, and the owner sees both frames before paying it.

**The Species fog stays in the shaders**, selectable per frame (`FogMode::LinearToColor`, to the fog colour in the constants, black by default), because the capture draws both for the comparison this ADR rests on and because the owner may override: every capture writes frame 100 under the linear fog and frame 200 under the desaturation from one vantage, so the comparison is re-made on every run.

**The far field is drawn, so the far plane and the depth mapping follow.** Under the linear fog nothing beyond 0.74 of the extent needed drawing; under the desaturation everything in the frustum does, and the triangle budget of `Client/TerrainPass.h` (a million, the near band at stride 1 and the rest at the stride that keeps the count) does the work the fog no longer does. Two consequences are settled here rather than met later:

- **The far plane scales with the landscape**: `Camera::SetFarPlane` takes `FAR_PLANE_EXTENT_FACTOR` (1.5) times the extent, never less than the Species 15,000 (`CAMERA_FAR`). The diagonal of a landscape is 1.42 of its extent and the camera may be `CAMERA_MAX_HEIGHT` above a corner; on Small the plane stays at 15,000, on Medium it is 24,576, on Large 49,152. A gray far field cut by a hard edge would be the worst of both choices.
- **The depth is reversed**: the projection maps the near plane to 1 and the far plane to 0 (`Camera::Projection` passes the planes swapped), the scene target clears its depth to `SCENE_DEPTH_CLEAR` (0), and every world pass tests `D3D12_COMPARISON_FUNC_GREATER` (`Client/SceneTarget.h`; the terrain and water pipelines). With the standard mapping a `D32_FLOAT` buffer resolves about 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ × d² / n world units at distance d for a near plane n: one unit at 6,480 units, which is where the water plane, nine under the sea level, and the dipped shore, ten under it, begin to fight, and 14 units at Medium's far plane. Reversed, the float's exponent carries the precision and the resolution is about 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ × d: a millimetre at 8,192 units, six at 49,152. The frustum planes are unchanged, because the two half-spaces 0 ≤ z and z ≤ w bound the same frustum whichever is the near one.

## Consequences

- The far field costs triangles instead of being fogged away; the budget, not the fog, bounds a frame. A Large landscape's 256 chunks all pass culling from a high vantage and draw at stride 8, which is what the budget is for; the count is logged per frame.
- The world does not fade to nothing. A biome's fog colour in `Biomes.json` (`SpeciesLook.md` §11) is carried for the linear mode and unused by the default; the desaturation has no colour to carry.
- A world pass that forgets the reversed depth tests `LESS` against a buffer cleared to 0 and draws nothing, which a capture shows at once; a pass that forgets the clear value draws everything over everything. The rule sits in `SceneTarget.h` beside the format.
- The far plane moves with the landscape, so a frustum-culling figure or a depth figure measured on Small does not carry to Large.
- **The two frames were drawn with no sky.** Nothing draws one yet: the sky is the black clear colour, as Species's is, and its three additive layers are `m2-skirmish/T8`. The evidence above is the horizon band, which is exactly the part of the frame those layers occupy, so the comparison is re-made with the sky drawn before it is final. `OpenQuestions.md` Q17 carries that, together with the sky's scaling, which this ADR did not settle.
- The owner has not seen the frames on a real display: this ADR is written on the statistics of two frames drawn by WARP. If the owner overrides at T22, the superseding ADR flips `DEFAULT_FOG_MODE` and nothing else moves: the far plane and the reversed depth are right under either fog.
- The unlit-and-unfogged slot puts a branch in every world pixel shader. It is one comparison on an interpolated constant.

## Measurements

- **The two frames**: CI run 39 (`a32e5d0`), the Windows job's capture step, `FrontierCommander --warp --capture 300 Captures` on the `windows-latest` image, WARP, the Debug build with the debug layer on and reporting nothing. The Small landscape of the built-in definition: 513 samples a side at a spacing of 16, extent 8,192, highest sample 130; 16 chunks built at four strides into 386,624 vertices and 664,912 triangles. Frame 100 and frame 200 are both from (983, 320, 983) looking at (4,506, 0, 4,506), 178,810 triangles and 16 water quads each; the far corner is 10,195 units from the camera. The frames are the run's `captures` artefact (https://github.com/Zwaliebaba/Frontier-Commander/actions/runs/35280657525/artifacts/10522038159) and `Tools/DescribeCapture.py`'s account of them is in its job log; the figures quoted above are that account's, over six horizontal bands of 180 rows, near-black meaning every channel under 8.
- **Whole-frame figures**, frame 100 against frame 200: near-black 50.9% against 48.3%; gray 8.5% against 11.1%; green 38.5% in both and yellow 2.0% in both. The upper two bands are sky in both (100% near-black); the third band, holding the horizon, is 99.4% against 89.7% near-black; the fourth is 5.7% against 0.0%; the fifth and sixth agree.
- **WARP frame time**: 300 frames in 27.7 seconds, 92 ms a frame at 1920×1080 with four samples; the hundred linear frames took 8.7 seconds and the hundred desaturated ones 8.3, so the mode costs nothing measurable. Generating the landscape took 0.13 seconds and building its mesh 0.38. A software rasteriser's figure says nothing about a GPU's; the owner's run (`m0-foundation/T22`) records the real one.
- **The depth resolutions** are arithmetic on the mapping, not measurements: the derivative of the standard mapping z = f/(f − n) × (1 − n/d) is about n/d², a float near 1 has an ulp of 2⁻²³, and the reversed mapping's value near the far plane is about n/d with an ulp of 2⁻²³ × n/d.
- **The fog ranges** per size are arithmetic on the fractions and `SIZE_CLASS_CELLS` × `SAMPLES_PER_CELL_EDGE` × `SAMPLE_SPACING_WORLD_UNITS`.
